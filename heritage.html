Our Heritage · ⴰⵎⵣⵔⵓⵢ

3,000 years
of free hands.

Before the Romans, before Islam, before the borders — the Imaziɣen lived across North Africa. From the Atlas to the Sahara, they made what their hands could carry: rugs, jewelry, leather, pottery, blades.

The word Amazigh means «free person». For 30 centuries, they have refused to forget that.

ⵣ
The Yaz Free man · Amazigh identity
"The mountain has taught us everything. The book only learned to write it down." — Berber proverb · Atlas Mountains

Every piece at Aṣli begins in the hands of an Amazigh artisan — most often a woman, in a cooperative in the High Atlas, the Anti-Atlas, or the Rif. The crafts she practices are older than Morocco itself.

The Beni Ourain rug? Woven by Berber tribes since the Paleolithic era. The brass lantern? Forged using techniques from the medieval Marinid era. The leather pouf? Stitched with a curved needle and waxed cotton thread, exactly as it was 800 years ago.

We did not invent any of this. We are only the bridge between makers and buyers — without warehouses, without middlemen, without rushing them.

The Tifinagh script

The alphabet of the free.

Tifinagh (ⵜⵉⴼⵉⵏⴰⵖ) is the indigenous writing system of the Amazigh people — used for over 2,500 years. In 2003, Morocco made it official alongside Arabic. Here are the symbols you'll see throughout our shop.

ⵣ
Yaz / Aza
"Free man"
The most recognized Amazigh symbol. Found on flags, jewelry, tattoos, and woven into rugs. It means freedom, identity, dignity.
ⴰⵚⵍⵉ
Aṣli
"Authentic"
Our name. Meaning: original, true, real, unadulterated. Used to distinguish handmade from factory-made.
ⴰⵣⵓⵍ
Azul
"Hello / Peace"
The universal Amazigh greeting. Said with the right hand on the heart. The respectful way to enter a workshop or a home.
ⵉⵎⴰⵣⵉⵖⵏ
Imaziɣen
"The free people"
What the Amazigh call themselves. Plural of Amazigh. Not "Berbers" — that's the Greek word, meaning «foreigners».
The four lands

Every region, its own craft.

ⴰⵟⵍⴰⵚ
The Atlas
Beni Ourain rugs, sheep wool, silver jewelry. Cold mountain villages where weaving keeps families warm and fed through winter.
ⴰⵔⵉⴼ
The Rif
Embroidered fabrics, woven baskets, blue-painted villages. The north coast tradition — softer colors, finer thread, Mediterranean influence.
ⵙⵓⵙ
The Souss
Argan oil, women's cooperatives, silver Tuareg jewelry. The land where the argan tree grows — found nowhere else on earth.
ⵜⵉⵏⵉⵔⵉ
The Sahara
Tuareg jewelry, indigo dyes, woven mats, leather camel saddles. The deepest blue — Tuareg blue — comes from these dunes.
A short history

30 centuries of making.

1000 BC

The first looms

The earliest known Berber rugs date to the Paleolithic. The symbols woven into them — diamonds, zigzags, hands — are still used by the same tribes today, in the same valleys.

600 BC

Tifinagh appears

The first Tifinagh inscriptions are carved into stones across North Africa. The script will survive every empire that follows — Phoenician, Roman, Arab, French — without ever being defeated.

800 AD

The Marinid leatherworks

Fes becomes the world capital of tanned leather. The chouara tannery — still operating today, the oldest in the world — uses the same vegetable dyes (henna, indigo, saffron, mint) as it did in the year 850.

1500s

Zellige tilework

The geometric tile mosaics of Morocco reach their peak in the Saadian dynasty. The 8-pointed star (used throughout this site) becomes the signature pattern, never copied successfully outside North Africa.

2011

Tamazight becomes official

After centuries of marginalization, the Amazigh language and Tifinagh script become official languages of Morocco alongside Arabic. The Royal Institute of Amazigh Culture (IRCAM) is founded.

2026
